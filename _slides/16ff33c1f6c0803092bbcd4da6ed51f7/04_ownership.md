## Making User Ownership Real

Today, Common Ground operates as a **not for profit association** in Switzerland. This is the foundation for transitioning towards a bi-cameral ownership and governance system for the platform and evolving network of communities and users thereon.

The **Common Ground Coop**, once formed, will be be a transnational European Cooperative Society or SCE for short, seated in the EU 🇪🇺 The Coop will take over the operations of Common Ground, while the Association will receive usage fees to pay dev salaries. 

![](/assets/images/notion/16ff33c1f6c0803092bbcd4da6ed51f7/6fa11fd8-6b0e-4a4f-bb4a-a629a97a2e65.png)

The COOP will **use 1-member-1-vote **governance, the Association operates with **1-token-1-vote governance**

![](/assets/images/notion/16ff33c1f6c0803092bbcd4da6ed51f7/c6556abe-cce6-4e10-8c72-6c878412090b.png)

The stakeholders in the coop are potentially all users of Common Ground, including its communities and ecosystems. The stakeholders in the DUNA are the founders & members of the CG Team, some of our angel investors as well as our ecosystem partners who choose to become premium supporters like LUKSO.

In both hostile and, even more so, friendly regulatory environments, a reliable legal structure operating under on-chain governance provides an important and complementary form of resilience to the Common Ground ecosystem. Unlike the static, expensive, and often dysfunctional governance models of yesterday’s crypto foundations, the structure we propose is novel, agile, and fundamentally participatory, enabling true ownership for users.

The bicameral design allows for progressive decentralization from the start, while resulting in an end state where the association could be dissolved once its funding and leadership aren’t necessary anymore and all relevant IP has been open sourced to the community or legally transferred to the user-run cooperative.
